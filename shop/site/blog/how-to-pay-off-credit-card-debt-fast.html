How to Pay Off Credit Card Debt Fast: 6 Steps That Actually Work

Credit card interest is usually the most expensive money you owe, so it deserves to be paid off first and fast. The single biggest lever is simple: pay more than the minimum every month. Here is how much it matters.

What a bigger payment does

A $5,000 balance at 22% APR, with no new purchases:

Monthly paymentMonths to pay offTotal interest
$15052$2,798
$25026$1,286
$40015$732

Going from $150 to $250 a month halves the time and saves over $1,500. That is why every step below is about freeing up money for the card.

6 steps

  1. Stop adding to the balance. Take the card out of your phone wallet and online shops. Use a debit card while you pay it down.
  2. List every debt. Write the balance, interest rate (APR) and minimum payment for each card and loan. You can't plan what you can't see.
  3. Pick an order. Highest interest first (the avalanche) saves the most money; smallest balance first (the snowball) gives quicker wins. See our snowball vs. avalanche example.
  4. Find a fixed extra amount. Look at your budget for $50–$200 a month: subscriptions, dining out, a side gig. Make it automatic on payday.
  5. Roll payments over. When one card is paid off, add its whole payment to the next one. Your total monthly payment stays the same, but each debt falls faster.
  6. Ask about a lower rate. Call your card issuer and ask for a lower APR, or compare 0% balance-transfer offers. Read the fees (often 3–5%) and the date the promotional rate ends before you move a balance.

Know your debt-free date

Motivation is easier when you can see the finish line. A payoff planner simulates every month, including the rollover, and shows the exact month each card is paid off and how much interest an extra $50 saves.
