How to Set Financial Goals for 2027 (And Actually Keep Them)

Most New Year money resolutions are too vague to keep. "Save more" and "get out of debt" don't tell you what to do on a Tuesday in March. This routine turns them into numbers and small weekly actions.

1. Look back at 2026 (30 minutes)

Pull up your bank statements and answer three questions: What did I earn? What did I spend, roughly by category? What did I save or pay off? You need a starting point before you can set a target.

2. Pick no more than three goals

Three goals you finish beat ten you forget. Good money goals are specific and have a number and a date:

  • "Build a $3,000 emergency fund by December 2027."
  • "Pay off the $4,200 credit card by August 2027."
  • "Save $1,800 for a summer trip by June 2027."

3. Turn each goal into a monthly number

Divide the amount by the months you have. $3,000 over 12 months is $250 a month; $1,800 over 6 months is $300. Add these to your budget as fixed lines, and automate the transfers on payday.

4. Plan the year, then the month, then the week

  • Year: mark the deadlines and the months with big bills (insurance, holidays).
  • Month: on the 1st, write the month's three priorities and the money moves for each goal.
  • Week: on Sunday, check last week's spending and list one action per goal.

5. Track a habit, not just a number

Small daily habits move the big numbers: no-spend days, packing lunch, logging expenses. A habit tracker that you tick every day keeps the goal in front of you.

6. Review every quarter

In April, July and October, compare your progress with the plan. Behind? Lower a target or extend a date instead of giving up. The goal is progress, not perfection.
