The 50/30/20 Budget Rule: How to Set Up a Monthly Budget That Works

The 50/30/20 rule is the simplest way to start a budget. Take your monthly take-home pay (after tax) and split it three ways:

  • 50% needs: rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, transport, minimum debt payments.
  • 30% wants: dining out, shopping, entertainment, subscriptions, travel.
  • 20% savings and extra debt payments: emergency fund, retirement, goals, paying debt faster.

Example: $4,650 a month

BucketShareAmount
Needs50%$2,325
Wants30%$1,395
Savings & debt20%$930

Then break each bucket into categories. For needs, that might be rent $1,450, utilities $220, groceries $520 and transport $260. Write a number next to every category. That number is your budget.

Step by step

  1. Find your real income. Use the amount that lands in your account, not your salary.
  2. List fixed costs first. Rent, insurance, phone and loan payments rarely change.
  3. Estimate variable costs from last month. Look at your bank statement for groceries, fuel and dining out.
  4. Pay yourself first. Set the savings transfer to happen on payday.
  5. Track every transaction. Once a week, log what you spent. It takes ten minutes.
  6. Review at month end. Where did you go over? Move money between categories for next month, not after the fact.

When 50/30/20 doesn't fit

In expensive cities, needs can take 60% or more. That's fine: shrink wants first, and keep savings above zero, even at 5%. If you have high-interest debt, push the third bucket toward debt until it's gone. The point is to choose your split instead of discovering it afterwards.
